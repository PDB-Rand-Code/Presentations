Why Oil and Electricity Prices are Linked: While the UK generates a large and growing share of power from renewables (wind, solar) and nuclear, thermal gas power stations (CCGT) still generate 30% to 40% of the UK's total electricity. Under the UK's "merit order" power market, gas-fired plants set the marginal clearing price for all wholesale power during most hours. Because global gas contracts, LNG shipping, and petroleum refining are closely linked to crude oil benchmarks, oil price spikes directly drive up electricity wholesale costs.

Why is There a Standing Charge?

The standing charge is a mandatory daily fee covering the fixed operational costs of keeping homes connected: maintaining physical wires, pipes, pylons, meter infrastructure, customer servicing, and government-mandated safety programmes. It applies regardless of energy consumption.

Government Consultation: Eliminating Gas Standing Charges

The UK government and Ofgem are reviewing proposals to remove the standing charge for domestic gas entirely. Under this reform, fixed network costs would be added to the volumetric price per unit (p/kWh), directly incentivising energy reduction and lowering costs for low-usage households.

Government Interventions: Electricity VAT Exemption & £100 Credit

Electricity VAT Exemption

Price per unit and standing charge figures are always quoted before VAT. VAT is added to the overall bill at the end based on your total consumption.

• Electricity Bills: The UK government has removed the standard 5% VAT on domestic electricity bills from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027 (0% VAT on bill total).
• Gas Bills: The standard 5% VAT charge continues to be added to the overall gas bill total.
*Costs cannot be compared directly to previous periods without accounting for this VAT change.

Why Winter Bills May Not Fall

Despite the electricity VAT exemption, households may not see much of a reduction in their overall winter bills due to two main factors:

1. Wholesale Gas Escalation: Ongoing conflict in the Middle East has kept international wholesale commodity purchase costs high.
2. Seasonal Demand Peak: Sub-zero winter heating consumption naturally offsets the percentage savings gained from the tax removal.

£100 Household Support Credit

The UK government announced that around 20 million households across Great Britain will receive £100 towards their energy bill starting from January 2027.

• Delivered automatically through supplier billing accounts.
• Designed as temporary support to help mitigate elevated winter wholesale rates.
Ofgem Policy Note: Published unit rates (32.69p electricity, 10.96p gas) exclude VAT. The removal of the 5% VAT on the final electricity bill provides relief, but higher winter usage and higher wholesale prices mean overall expenditure remains substantial.

Strategic Outlook: Evaluating the Risks of Fixing vs. Variable

Unpredictable Market Drivers

We cannot predict the future. Energy pricing heading into Spring 2027 depends on several external and unpredictable factors:

• Crude Oil Movements: We do not know whether the price of crude oil will continue to rise or begin to drop.
• Geopolitical Situation: Will the war in the Middle East continue or ease? These all play a direct factor in the wholesale price of energy.
• April 2027 Outlook: Hopefully wholesale prices will drop in April as warmer weather arrives, but it remains an unknown.

A Balanced Perspective: Risk to Fix vs. Risk to Stay

Both choices carry distinct risks that should be weighed carefully:

• The Risk of Leaving It on Variable: If wholesale gas and oil prices remain high or escalate further, you remain exposed to elevated price cap rates.
• The Risk of Fixing: If the price drops in April, you could end up paying a higher rate than the new cap or have to pay the £50 early exit fee per fuel to leave the fix.
Strategic Summary: It is a risk to leave your tariff on the standard variable cap and a risk to fix. Each household needs to decide whether they value guaranteed price certainty through the peak winter months, while factoring in the potential £50 per fuel exit fee if rates drop in April.
