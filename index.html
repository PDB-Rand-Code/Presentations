Why Oil and Electricity Prices are Linked: While the UK generates a large and growing share of power from renewables (wind, solar) and nuclear, thermal gas power stations (CCGT) still generate 30% to 40% of the UK's total electricity. Under the UK's "merit order" power market, gas-fired plants set the marginal clearing price for all wholesale power during most hours. Because global gas contracts, LNG shipping, and petroleum refining are closely linked to crude oil benchmarks, oil price spikes directly drive up electricity wholesale costs.

What was the Energy Price Guarantee (EPG)?

In Autumn 2022, following the invasion of Ukraine and international wholesale price shocks, Ofgem's standard formula produced an uncapped annual bill of £4,279. To protect consumers, the UK government introduced the EPG, directly subsidising suppliers to hold the electricity unit rate to 34.04p/kWh. January 2027 (32.69p) is the highest rate seen since that crisis intervention.

Supplier of Last Resort (SoLR) Levy Spike

Electricity standing charges jumped from 24.89p/day (2022) to over 54p–60p/day today because nearly 30 energy suppliers collapsed in 2021/2022. The ~£2.7bn cost of transferring customers and protecting credit balances was placed onto electricity standing charges. Industry levies and true-up repayments are scheduled to conclude around 2027/2028.

What was the Energy Price Guarantee (EPG)?

The EPG was the UK government's emergency intervention introduced in Autumn 2022 to shield households after Russia invaded Ukraine. Without government subsidies, wholesale gas prices would have driven annual dual-fuel bills past £4,279. The EPG capped the domestic gas unit rate to 10.33p/kWh.

Historical Record: January 2027 Exceeds 2023 Crisis

At 10.96p/kWh, the confirmed January 2027 gas price cap is 0.63p higher than the January 2023 EPG crisis cap (10.33p). This makes January 2027 the single highest regulated domestic winter gas unit rate on record in the UK.

Why are Standing Charges Higher Than Pre-Crisis Levels?

Prior to 2022, electricity standing charges were ~25p/day. The collapse of ~30 energy suppliers triggered Ofgem's Supplier of Last Resort (SoLR) process. Over £2.7bn in customer transfer and unhedged debt costs was added directly onto standing charges. Multi-year recovery levies are scheduled to conclude around 2027/2028.

Government Consultation: Eliminating Gas Standing Charges

The UK government and Ofgem are reviewing proposals to remove the standing charge for domestic gas entirely. Under this reform, fixed network maintenance costs would be moved to the unit rate (p/kWh), directly rewarding energy reduction and cutting bills for low-usage households.

Government Interventions & Consumer Protections

Electricity VAT Exemption

The UK government has removed the standard 5% VAT strictly from domestic electricity bills between 1 October 2026 and 30 June 2027.

• Electricity: Quoted 32.69p/kWh rate excludes VAT (0% VAT on bill).
• Gas: 5% VAT will be charged on top of your combined usage and standing charge for the billing period.

£150 Bill Rebate & Winter Fuel

Eligible pensioner and low-income households receive the government's £150 Warm Home Discount, credited directly by energy suppliers onto electricity accounts between October and March.

• Paid directly to energy suppliers to reduce winter bills.
• Operates alongside DWP Winter Fuel Payments.

£100 Household Support Credit

Around 20 million households across Great Britain will receive an automatic £100 credit applied toward their energy bills starting from January 2027.

• Applied automatically through supplier accounts.
• Designed as temporary relief against peak winter rates.
The Priority Services Register (PSR): Essential Protection for Vulnerable Residents

The Priority Services Register (PSR) is a free, confidential service provided by all UK energy suppliers and network operators. Eligible residents include pensionable-age customers, households with young children under 5, individuals with disabilities or long-term health conditions, and anyone relying on powered medical equipment.

Strategic Outlook: Fixed vs Variable

When is the Best Time to Fix?

Reviewing the past 5 years of Ofgem price caps shows that energy rates consistently hit their annual low during the warmer spring and summer periods (specifically the April to June and July to September cap windows) as European heating demand falls:

• Post-Crisis Lows: July–September 2024 saw unit rates drop to 22.36p (electric) and 5.48p (gas).
• Spring 2026 Dip: April 2026 rates dipped to 24.67p (electric) and 5.74p (gas).
• Seasonal Recommendation: Historically, locking into a 12-month fixed deal during the April to June price cap window has secured the lowest baseline rates, whereas fixing during peak winter runs the risk of locking in inflated seasonal wholesale rates.

A Balanced Perspective: Risk vs Certainty

Neither forecasters nor suppliers can predict wholesale markets with certainty. Both paths carry distinct trade-offs:

• Risk of Staying on Variable: If wholesale gas and oil prices remain high, you remain exposed to elevated price caps throughout 2027.

• Risk of Fixing: If global wholesale rates drop in April 2027, you could be tied into higher rates or need to pay an early exit fee (e.g. £50 per fuel) to leave.
Market Outlook & Guidance Note: Energy pricing heading into Spring 2027 remains heavily dependent on unpredictable external variables—including global crude oil movements ($84+/bbl), ongoing Middle East geopolitical tensions, and European winter storage depletion. While historical 5-year trends indicate that spring and summer cap periods typically offer the lowest baseline for 12-month fixes, past performance cannot guarantee future wholesale outcomes. Households should assess their personal consumption and budget certainty preferences.
